ROAS vs MER: which one to trust

ROAS (return on ad spend) is what each ad platform shows you: the revenue it attributes to its own ads, divided by what you spent there. Google Ads, Meta and TikTok each count every sale their ads touched within their attribution window, so when a customer saw a Meta ad, clicked a Google ad and then bought, both platforms claim the same order.

MER (marketing efficiency ratio), also called blended ROAS, ignores attribution. It takes the revenue your store actually made and divides it by everything you spent on ads:

MER = total store revenue ÷ total ad spend

Platform ROAS is useful for comparing campaigns inside one platform. MER tells you whether your marketing as a whole pays off, and it can't be inflated by double counting.

Break-even ROAS

Revenue isn't profit. To know whether an MER is good, compare it with the ROAS at which your ads exactly pay for themselves:

Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin

Gross margin here is what is left of a sale after product cost, shipping and payment fees. With a 45% margin, break-even ROAS is 1 ÷ 0.45 ≈ 2.2. Below that, every sale your ads bring costs you money; above it, the difference is what is left for your other costs and profit.

  • Margin 70% → break-even ROAS 1.4
  • Margin 50% → break-even ROAS 2.0
  • Margin 30% → break-even ROAS 3.3

Cost per order

Blended CPA is your total ad spend divided by all orders. Compare it with the most you can pay for an order and still break even:

Break-even cost per order = average order value × gross margin

Why platforms report more than your store made

Add up the revenue each platform reports and you will often get more than your store's real revenue. That gap is double counting, plus view-through conversions and long attribution windows. The calculator above shows it as a percentage: the bigger it is, the less you can rely on any single platform's ROAS to decide where your budget goes.

Questions

ROAS, MER and break-even in short.

What is the difference between ROAS and MER?

ROAS is the revenue one ad platform attributes to its own ads, divided by the spend on that platform. MER (marketing efficiency ratio, also called blended ROAS) is your total store revenue divided by your total ad spend on all platforms. MER does not depend on attribution, so a sale is never counted twice.

How do I calculate break-even ROAS?

Break-even ROAS is 1 divided by your gross margin. With a 40% margin, break-even ROAS is 1 / 0.40 = 2.5: every unit of ad spend must bring 2.5 units of revenue before the ads pay for themselves.

Why do my ad platforms report more revenue than my store made?

Each platform counts every sale its ads touched within its attribution window. When a customer saw a Meta ad, clicked a Google ad and then bought, both platforms count the same order. Added together, platform-reported revenue is often higher than the revenue in your store.

What is a good MER?

A good MER is one above your break-even ROAS with enough room left for your other costs and profit. It depends on your margins: a brand with a 70% gross margin breaks even at an MER of about 1.4, a brand with a 30% margin needs about 3.3.
